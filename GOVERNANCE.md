# Governance

How arandu.ai decides things. This document restates the constitution's rules on voting, voters, and moderation. It does not add to them or soften them. Where it differs from [the constitution](metasystemic/CONSTITUTION.md), the constitution governs.

arandu.ai is a public civic instrument. Governance exists to keep the lens honest, not to settle what the numbers mean. Maintainers steward the constitution, not the conclusions.

## When a vote is required

A vote is required for any of the following:

- adding a canonical tab
- removing a canonical tab
- renaming a canonical tab
- changing the order or public meaning of canonical tabs
- changing the constitution
- changing voting rights
- changing maintainer powers

These are metasystemic changes. They change the lens, not only the picture. Systemic changes — a new card, a new query, a new data source, a correction, a better label — do not require a vote. They are the daily life of the project and should be welcomed.

## Proposal lifecycle

A metasystemic change must include a written proposal, a reason, a bias impact note, a migration plan, a rollback plan, and a public discussion period.

A proposal must stay open for at least 14 days before a vote.

During that period, contributors may ask for:

- data
- alternatives
- risks
- examples

The proposer should answer these in the open. Discussion is part of the record.

## The passing bar

To pass, a proposal needs all of the following:

- at least 5 eligible voters
- at least 2 maintainer approvals
- a two-thirds majority of eligible votes
- no unresolved reproducibility objection
- no unresolved governance objection

All five conditions must hold. A two-thirds majority does not carry a proposal if a reproducibility or governance objection remains unresolved.

## Provisional decisions

If fewer than 5 eligible voters exist, maintainers may pass a provisional decision.

A provisional decision expires after 90 days unless it is ratified.

This is a temporary measure for a small project, not a way around the bar. Once enough eligible voters exist, a provisional decision should be brought to a full vote.

## Voter eligibility

Voting power comes from contribution, not ideology. Eligible voters are people who have shown care for the project.

A contributor becomes eligible after any one of the following:

- two merged pull requests
- one merged data source contribution
- one accepted methodology review
- one accepted governance proposal
- maintainer invitation based on public project work

## One identity, one voter

No person may vote with more than one identity.

## The 40 percent organization cap

No organization may control more than 40 percent of counted votes on a metasystemic decision.

This is not to block disagreement. Disagreement is healthy. This is to block coups.

## Capture pause and governance review

If coordinated capture is suspected, maintainers may pause the vote and open a governance review.

The pause exists to protect the process from capture, not to protect any outcome from criticism.

## Moderation: bad method is not allowed

Moderators and maintainers must protect the project from:

- uncited claims
- cherry-picked datasets
- misleading chart scales
- partisan framing
- source laundering
- methodological opacity
- spam
- harassment
- coordinated capture
- low-quality automated pull requests

Moderators must not block a contribution because they dislike its political implications.

A chart may be uncomfortable. A number may be inconvenient. A trend may disturb a preferred story. That is allowed. Bad method is not.

## Maintainers

Maintainers steward the constitution, not the conclusions. They count votes, approve metasystemic proposals, may pass provisional decisions when fewer than 5 eligible voters exist, may pause a vote and open a governance review if coordinated capture is suspected, and uphold the moderation standard above.
